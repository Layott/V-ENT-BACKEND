## 1. Formations: eight to twenty-eight

`vent_cards/formations.py` now holds every shape EA ships, with its real name
including the lettered variants that are genuinely different shapes rather
than the same one renamed: `4-3-3 (2)` through `(5)`, `4-4-2 (2)`,
`4-2-3-1 (2)`, `4-1-2-1-2 (2)`, `4-5-1 (2)`, `3-5-2 (2)`.

One list, on the server. The picker draws the pitch from the coordinates the
server sends, so **adding a formation needs no drawing code in the frontend**
and the picker can never offer one the server would refuse.

`vent_cards/tests_formations.py`, 18 tests. Two of them earn their place:

**The first number of the name must equal the defenders IN THE BACK BAND.**
Written naively it counts positions, and it immediately failed on `3-5-2 (2)`,
which carries LWB and RWB. In that shape those are two of the five in midfield,
and their depth on the pitch says so. Counting by position alone called it a
back five. The test now requires a defensive position AND `y < 40`.

**No two slots closer than about an eighth of the pitch in BOTH directions.**
A card has a size: it is drawn at roughly an eighth of the pitch's width and is
1.4 times as tall as it is wide. The goalkeeper's card sat on top of the middle
centre back in every back three and every back five. Distinct coordinates,
green tests, and visible only in a browser. Fixed by moving the middle centre
back from y=19 to y=21/22 and shrinking the pitch card from 92px to 80px with
the pitch at 5/7 rather than 3/4.

## 2. The screens that did not exist

I marked inbox rows 28, 29 and 30 done on 3 September with a backend and no
screen. The endpoints for submitting, for the organiser's decision and for the
squad rules were built, tested and green, and there was nowhere to press any of
them. The picker had one Save button.

### `LineupPicker.js`, the player's side

- **Submit squad**, a separate control from **Save draft**, because saving and
  handing in are different acts. Saving is a draft; submitting is the moment it
  is theirs to be judged on.
- The rules and where this squad stands against them, **above the pitch**, so
  somebody knows what they are building to before they build it.
- **Why a disabled Submit is disabled, said next to it.** "Save the draft
  first", "Fix what is listed above", "Already submitted. Change anything and it
  goes back for checking." A greyed-out control tells nobody anything.
- 28 formations in three groups by back line. Twenty-eight loose chips is a
  wall rather than a choice.
- A `dirty` flag: submitting reads the SAVED squad, so handing in with an
  unsaved change would submit the old one. Submit saves first when dirty.

### `SquadStatus.js`, shared by both sides

The rules, the spend against the budget, the state (draft, submitted, accepted,
sent back) and the organiser's reason. **Every line is built from a CODE the
server sent plus its numbers**, never from a sentence written in Python, which
cannot be translated. `OVER_BUDGET` with `spent`, `allowed` and `over` becomes
"This eleven costs 240,000 coins. The limit is 200,000, so you are 40,000
over." in whichever language the reader has.

### `SquadReviewPanel.js`, the organiser's side

The rules editor, then the queue with **Accept** and **Send back** on each row,
ordered submitted-first because that is the work. A rejection asks for a reason
and will not send without one: "no" with nothing after it is a message the
player cannot act on and they will ask anyway. The queue also says, where an
organiser looking at an empty list will read it, that **with no rules set
nothing can be submitted at all**.

### The rule this obeys

Nothing in the browser works out whether a squad is legal. `POST /lineup/` now
returns `violations`, `spend` and `squad_rules` alongside the lineup, so the
screen that has just saved knows where it stands from the same engine the
submit endpoint uses. A copy of the rules in JavaScript is the rules written
twice, and the two copies eventually disagree.

## 3. The card

`FutCard` drew a rating, a position and a name over whatever Futbin's CDN
returned, and hid the stats at the size the pitch actually uses. So every card
on the pitch was a flat gold band with a number on it.

Worse: **`cdn.futbin.com/design/img/cards/tiny/<item_type>.png` answers 404**
for every item type. Checked with curl. So the whole design was one failed
request away from nothing, and it failed every time.

Now the card is drawn here, from the data: rating, position, nation as three
letters, portrait, name, club and **all six stats**, with no network at all.
Futbin's frame art is a bonus layer on top when it happens to load. A
goalkeeper shows DIV HAN KIC REF SPE POS rather than PAC, decided by which six
the card actually carries rather than by its position string.

**One layout at every size.** `--fut-w` is the width, the font size is derived
from it, and every measurement inside is in `em`. The pitch, the bench and the
search results each set that one variable, so a 54px card on a phone pitch and
a 104px card in a search result are the same rules and cannot drift apart.

No gradients anywhere: one flat fill per item type, with the stats on a flat
panel one step darker. That is surface and space, which is what the design
rules ask for, and a metallic sweep reads as mud at 54px.

## 5. Where a graphic sits on the frame

> "SHould also be able to move the position of overlays, whether they load in
> at the centre bottom or center top, or top right or top left or middle or
> middle right, etc. this mostly affect lower thirds."

Asked twice, so it is built.

### The contract

`vent_tournament/presentation.py` gains three options beside entry, exit, hold
and duration:

| | |
|---|---|
| `position` | a nine point grid: `top_left` `top_centre` `top_right` `middle_left` `centre` `middle_right` `bottom_left` `bottom_centre` `bottom_right`, plus `as_designed` |
| `offset_x`, `offset_y` | a nudge off that anchor in pixels at 1920x1080, limited to plus or minus 800 |

They merge the way everything else in that module does: the broadcast sets a
house style once and any single graphic may differ, with the element's word
winning. An element can also say `as_designed` to come back to where it was
drawn, so setting a house style cannot trap one graphic away from its own place.

### The dangerous part, and it is not the nine positions

**The default is `as_designed`, and it adds no class at all.** Any other
default would have moved every graphic already on air the moment this shipped,
silently, during somebody's live broadcast.
`tests_presentation_position.py` opens with four tests holding exactly that,
and `as_designed` is first in the list as well as the default so a console that
renders the list in order offers it first.

### How it moves without editing twenty elements

Every studio element anchors itself with its own `position: absolute` and its
own `left`/`top`. The position rules are appended at the **end** of
`studio.module.css`, and CSS Modules keep file order, so a later rule of equal
specificity wins. One pair of rules moves any element, with no `!important` and
no edit to a single element.

The child's inset is cleared first (`top/right/bottom/left: auto`), because an
element that anchored itself with `right` would otherwise fight the new anchor
and stretch across the frame.

The safe area is 5 per cent, which is 96px across and 54px down at 1920x1080.
Broadcasters cut into the edges and a lower third flush to the bottom loses its
descenders.

### The console

A **Sits** select on every element and on the house style, built from the
server's own `presentation_options.positions`. The console holds the WORDS for
each name but never the LIST, and a test enforces that: a second list is a
position an operator can pick and the server then refuses. The nudge appears
only once a graphic has been moved, so nobody is shown a control that would do
nothing.

### Uploaded overlays

They already have this, and it is better handled where it is. An organiser's own
HTML is their design and one CSS rule moves it, so what was missing was telling
them where things belong. The designer prompt now carries a **WHERE IT SITS ON
THE FRAME** section: the two anchoring patterns, the 96px and 54px safe area,
where a lower third, a score bar and a sponsor wall each belong, and a warning
against anchoring all four edges, which stretches a graphic across the frame the
moment a name is longer than the placeholder. Four tests pin it.

### Verified in the browser

A real `lower_third` on a live session at `localhost:3005`:

| Setting | Measured |
|---|---|
| nothing set | `left: 80, bottom: 689`, and the stage carries **no position class** |
| `top_right` | `right: 1658` of a 1745 viewport, exactly 87px clear, which is the 5 per cent |
| `bottom_centre` with `offset_y: -60` | centred on 873 against a viewport midpoint of 873, `bottom: 38.86px` plus `matrix(1,0,0,1,-154.425,-60)`, so 99px off the bottom |

28 tests in `tests_presentation_position.py`, and `vent_tournament` is green at
958.

One thing worth knowing for the next person: the first reading of that middle
row was wrong because the entrance animation was still playing when the
measurement was taken. Measure a positioned graphic from the computed style,
not from a bounding rect during the first half second.

## Why the same fault reached the CEO three times

> "i thought a check was built for frontend so that any backend design that
> needs a frontend ui, gets the ui built. we hvae seen this eror like 3 times
> now. why is it still happening?"

Three separate causes. Only one of them was the checker being wrong.

### Cause 1: the check was too loose to catch it

`endpoint-callers.py` joined every frontend file into one blob and asked
whether each literal segment of a route appeared in it. On a codebase this size
almost every word appears somewhere, so `tournament/<id>/lineup/submit/` was
reported as **called** because `lineup` is in the picker and `submit` is in the
KYC page. Two unrelated files, and the checker said the endpoint had a caller.

The unit is a **file** now:

1. the route's last literal segment must be **used as a path** in one file
   (a path boundary on its left, a slash, query or quote on its right, so a
   word in a comment or a variable of the same name does not count), and
2. every other literal segment must appear in **that same file**.

The app mount prefix is dropped, because `StudioPanel` serves tournaments and
events from one component and writes `${API}/${kind}/${ref}/studio${path}`, so
the literal word `tournament` is nowhere in it.

**This closes gate E2, which 3 September recorded as ABANDONED.** That note
said a strict rule cannot be grepped: requiring segments in order on one line
reported 49 false orphans, and dropping the prefix still reported 22. It was
right about one line and wrong about the conclusion. One line is too strict
because helpers hold half a path; the whole frontend is too loose; **the file
the caller lives in is neither**, and that middle ground was never tried.

### Cause 2: nothing ran it

`check-all.py` is the one command that gets run, and this checker was not in
it. **Neither were twenty others** that had been written, tested and left on
disk: `check-error-ui`, `check-event-tabs`, `check-hover-lift`,
`check-inert-controls`, `check-language-blocks`, `check-legal`,
`check-null-images`, `check-pollers`, `check-stale-comingsoon`,
`check-status-buckets`, `check-tdz`, `check-unbounded-await`,
`check-format-catalogue`, `check-required-fields`, `check-entrant-branches`,
`check-overlay-runtime`, `check-guides`, `check-tabstrips`, `check-user-chips`,
`check-embeds`.

All 30 are in it now, blocking where they stand at zero and reported as debt
where they do not. A catcher outside that table depends on somebody
remembering, which is the thing catchers exist to replace.

### Cause 3: there were two copies of it

One at `V-ENT/tools/` and one at `V-ENT-BACKEND/tools/`, **drifted by a day and
a half**, with the documentation naming both: `GATES.md` and
`tasks/rules/STRUCTURAL-RULES.md` say `cd V-ENT && python tools/...`, and
`GATES-SEP02.md` says `cd V-ENT-BACKEND && python tools/...`. Which answer you
got depended on which directory you were in. I spent twenty minutes improving
the root copy before noticing it was the stale one.

The workspace root is not a git repository, so anything kept only there is lost
with the machine. The backend copy is the only copy now; the root files are
six-line forwarders through `tools/_forward.py` so every command already
written down keeps working; and `tools/check-duplicate-checkers.py` fails if a
real second copy appears again. That is the same-list-in-two-places fault for
the fourth recorded time, after the format catalogue, the event console tabs
and the five label maps.

### Both new checkers self-test

`endpoint-callers.py --self-test`: 9 fixtures, each a real shape. The fault
that shipped (words in unrelated files), the whole path in one template, a base
URL in a variable, a helper holding half the path, a path in a constants file, a
single common segment, a word in a comment, and two routes differing only by a
trailing segment.

`check-duplicate-checkers.py --self-test`: 4 fixtures. A forwarder, a second
copy, a forwarder that grew a tail, and a root-only tool that is not a
duplicate.

A checker reporting 0 means "clean" OR "broken", and only a self-test tells
them apart.
